Workbench basics

UDL means Uniformly Distributed Load: the weight a worktop can carry when it is spread evenly from end to end. Concentrate the same weight in one spot and the safe limit drops.

Three words people use as if they mean the same thing. Here is what each one includes, and why buying a complete system makes storage, power and material flow fit together.

Three workbench series built for three different jobs. How to match Base, Scitec and Pro to general assembly, labs and heavy-duty work.

What holds up your bench can also hold your tools. When to keep the space open with a leg frame, and when a base cabinet should carry the worktop.

Over a long span, steady weight and vibration can make a worktop bow. A pair of steel rails under the top keeps it flat.

Factory floors are rarely flat. How levelling glides and stud levellers stop a bench rocking and keep measurements true.

Choose the worktop by what touches it. Which surface suits assembly, electronics, labs, wet areas and heavy work.

The most common lab question, answered in one minute: when high-pressure laminate is enough, and when you need stainless steel.

ESD protection

They look alike from across the room but protect different things. A rubber mat protects parts from impact; an ESD mat protects electronics from static.

A spark too small to see or feel can kill or weaken a chip. How static builds up, and why a damaged board can pass today and fail later.

Two ways to make a bench ESD-safe. When to build it into the bench with a PELW worktop, and when a removable mat keeps the bench flexible.

An ESD kit only works if every link is connected. The chain from mat to connector to wrist strap to earth, step by step.

A safe bench is only one layer. How worktops, wrist straps, footwear and flooring work together to protect boards as they move.

Link and Flexi systems

A steel frame that mounts on your existing worktop and turns a flat bench into a complete workstation with shelves, bins, power and light.

Building a long line? A starter frame stands alone; an adder shares an upright with its neighbour, saving cost and legroom.

Same family, two different tools. One is a self-supporting station with its own worktop; the other is a freestanding frame for accessories.
