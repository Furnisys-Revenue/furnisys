Choosing the right workbench

Which Furnisys series is right for my application?

Start with the task and the load, then the environment. Many setups combine two or three series, for example a Scitec bench with a Link Uprighter above it and a mobile cabinet beside it.

Series Best for Construction Load capacity
Base Series General assembly, packing, training, office-side benches MS 2" x 2" tubular legs with levelling glides; linear or L-shape 454 kg UDL
Scitec Series Electronics, labs, testing, assembly with cabinets underneath 16–14 gauge MS 2" x 2" legs, base cabinets, or both 454 kg UDL
Pro Series Heavy, rough work: vices, grinders, test machines 16–14 gauge welded one-piece legs, 2.5" wide; six heights Our heaviest-duty series
Link System Shelves, power, lighting and tool storage above any bench; standalone workstands, trolleys, flow racks Heavy-gauge MS uprights slotted every 1" Workstation 250 kg UDL; flow rack 800 kg UDL
Flexi System Multi-user lines that will grow or change shape Double-sided columns with macro (3") and micro (1") slots Flow rack 630 kg UDL
Link Packing Accessories Packing and dispatch stations Added to Scitec, Pro, base cabinets and Link Deep shelves 68 kg UDL

UDL means uniformly distributed load: the weight spread evenly across the surface.

What is the difference between the Base and Scitec Series?

Both carry 454 kg UDL. Scitec can stand on base cabinets instead of legs, and takes privacy panels, instrument riser shelves with built-in power, and telescopic bottom shelves. Base is the simpler multipurpose bench, and is the one available in an L-shape.

When do I need the Pro Series?

Choose Pro when the bench will take impact, vibration or concentrated loads: bench vices, grinders, test machines or heavy castings. It comes in six heights from 700 to 1000 mm and takes GI- or stainless-steel-clad tops.

Example

An oil and gas R&D lab mounts its Universal Testing Machine on a Pro bench with a stainless steel top.

Load capacity and heavy machines

How much weight can a Furnisys workbench carry?

Base and Scitec benches carry 454 kg UDL, and Pro is built for heavier duty. If your load is concentrated, for example a machine at one end, share its weight and footprint with us. We will place the legs and support rails to suit.

Does a long workbench need extra support?

Often, yes. We add support in three cases:

  • Worktops 84" (2134 mm) and wider get an additional leg.

  • On Pro benches, a worktop support rail is added where legs or cabinets are more than 1000 mm apart.

  • On Scitec benches with leg frames, top support rails come as standard.

Can I mount a vibrating or high-force machine on the bench?

Yes. Use a Pro or Scitec bench with a GI- or stainless-steel-clad top, a top support rail and levellers. For the heaviest machines, give the machine its own bench with nothing stored underneath.

Example

For a Universal Testing Machine, an oil and gas lab uses two benches. The rear bench carries only the machine. The front bench, with two base cabinets and a stainless steel top, holds fixtures and tools.

Example

Valve-finishing benches with 38 mm GI-clad tops and reinforced support rails carry bench-mounted grinders without flexing.

Worktop materials

Which worktop should I choose?

Choose by what will touch the surface: general handling, static, chemicals, heat, or sparks and abrasion.

Worktop Thickness Best for Available on
Postform laminated particle board (PLW) 36 mm General assembly, packing and maintenance; the most economical choice All series
Postform electrostatic laminate (PELW) 36 mm Electronics and other static-sensitive work Base, Scitec, Link, Flexi
High-pressure laminate (HPL) 16 mm Labs with solvents, mild acids and frequent spills Base, Scitec, Flexi
Stainless steel clad (SSW) 38 mm Heat, corrosion and hygiene: pharma, food, machine mounting Base, Scitec, Pro, Flexi
GI clad (GW) 38 mm Impact, sparks, oil, grinding and heavy assembly Base, Scitec, Pro
Is HPL enough for a lab, or do I need stainless steel?

HPL suits dry and semi-wet labs where chemicals are handled often but are not highly aggressive. It costs less than stainless steel and is easy to clean. Choose stainless steel where there is heat, corrosive exposure or a hygiene requirement.

Example

An oil and gas materials lab uses HPL benches for its FTIR, DSC and TGA instruments, and a stainless steel bench for its hot mount press and grinder.

Can I protect the worktop from tools and dropped parts?

Yes. Add a 4–6 mm black rubber mat over any worktop. It absorbs tool impact, stops parts rolling and protects machined surfaces.

Example

A food plant's pump-gearbox repair bench and a rocket-engine prototyping lab both use rubber mats over their worktops.

ESD-safe workstations

How do you make a workstation ESD-safe?

We offer two routes:

  1. An electrostatic laminate (PELW) worktop with ESD accessories. Best when the bench will always be used for electronics.

  2. A standard laminate worktop with a 2 mm grey ESD mat and accessories. More economical, and the bench can return to general use later.

The ESD kit includes a wrist strap, grounding cords, buttons, a grounding plug and a 3-way connector. Flexi workstations can also take ground blocks, ground kits and a common-point ground block.

Example

An aerospace R&D team uses Flexi workstations with PELW worktops for PCB debugging, and a Base bench with an ESD mat in its coding area.

Which series is best for electronics assembly and testing?

Scitec is the usual choice. It takes ESD worktops, instrument riser shelves with power built into the supports, privacy panels and base cabinets. Flexi suits multi-user electronics lines, and Link workstands suit light, economical test stations.

Example

A medical-device testing lab runs 7 Scitec workstations with instrument risers and power, and 4 Scitec benches with base cabinets, all grounded.

What accessories does an electronics bench usually need?

  • Pegboard with tool holders, and a backstop so small parts don't roll off

  • Horizontal or vertical power rail for soldering stations, scopes and power supplies

  • Instrument riser or adjustable shelf to lift equipment off the work surface

  • LED undershelf light or fixed tube light for soldering and inspection

  • Bin rail for components, and hanging drawers or base cabinets for tools

  • Monitor arm where test data is read on screen

Do you supply ESD flooring?

ESD flooring is outside our product range. We can recommend a flooring provider, so your protection runs from the bench to the floor.

The Base Series is also available in an L-shape, with a 2- or 3-piece top in left- or right-hand orientation.

Can the working height be adjusted?

Yes, in steps:

  • Base: leg extenders in 2" steps up to 6", for an overall height of 32" to 42".

  • Scitec: leg extenders in 1" steps up to 6".

  • Pro: six leg heights to choose from.

  • Flexi: the worktop moves in 3" steps on the uprights, so one frame can suit seated or standing work.

What height should I choose?

  • Seated work (labelling, soldering, documentation): 750–850 mm.

  • Standing work (carton sealing, weighing, heavy assembly): 900–1100 mm.

  • Keep frequently used tools within 450–500 mm of the operator, and less-used items within 1000 mm.

Can you build to my exact dimensions?

Share your dimensions and we'll first check them against our standard sizes. Standard configurations are quicker to deliver and more economical; non-standard sizes take longer.

Example

A clean-tech manufacturer asked for made-to-size tables, and chose our standard 72" x 36" x 30" workstations for faster deployment.

Can trolley shelves be angled so operators don't bend?

Yes. Variable-angle shelves sit flat or tilted at 5° to 25° in 5° steps, at any height on the frame.

Example

A shirt manufacturer's dispatch team loads cartons onto angled trolley shelves at standing height, so operators no longer bend for each box.

What lighting options are available?

  • Fixed tube light with metal reflector

  • Magnetic LED undershelf task light, for under a shelf or cabinet

How can I organise tools and parts at the station?

  • Pegboards with 1/4" holes, taking standard hooks, plus screwdriver, Allen-key and spanner holders and trays

  • Marker boards and tack boards for SOPs and shift instructions

  • Bin rails, flat or at 15°, for fasteners and components

  • Tool rails, including swing-arm rails and spring balancers for heavy drivers

  • Swing-arm shelves: equipment shelf, accessory shelf, document holder and bin container

  • Computer accessories: monitor arm, keyboard tray and CPU holder

  • Packing accessories: roll holders, label holders and shelf dividers

Example

Can a bench vice be fitted?

Yes, on Pro and Scitec benches. We keep hanging cabinets at the opposite end from the vice.

Modularity and future expansion

Can I upgrade benches I already own?

Yes. The Link Uprighter mounts on the surface of Base, Pro and Scitec benches, and on most existing workbenches at your site. Once fitted, it carries shelves, pegboards, power rails, lights and bin rails, so you can upgrade without replacing the bench. Uprighter frames come in 24" to 60" widths to suit your worktop.

What are starter and adder frames?

A starter frame stands on its own; each adder frame shares an upright with the frame beside it. You need fewer frames for a long run, and can add bays later without replacing the line.

Example

An electrical-components packer uses one starter and one adder for a long, uninterrupted packing top with more legroom.

Can the layout change when my team grows?

Yes, especially with the Flexi System. Its double-sided columns serve two workstations back to back, and lines can be arranged in-line, back-to-back, or in U, L, T or X shapes.

Example

A jewellery manufacturer doubled from 5 to 10 operators by reconfiguring its in-line Flexi workstations back-to-back. It added only a work surface and a few shelves.

Example

An EV-charger R&D lab seats 12 engineers on Flexi workstations arranged back to back.

Can we assemble or relocate the furniture ourselves?

Link Workstands are designed for easy in-house assembly. Our benches can be dismantled and moved, and levellers make them easy to re-set on a new floor.

Example

An electronics startup assembled its five Link test stations itself and can rearrange them as production changes.

Planning, installation and delivery

What do you need from me to recommend a setup?

The fastest projects start with four things:

  1. The task at each station, and the equipment that will sit on it

  2. The load, and whether operators sit or stand

  3. Your room layout or dimensions

  4. Any reference photos or drawings

If you don't have all of these, we'll ask a few follow-up questions or visit your site.

Do you help plan the layout?

Yes. We prepare 2D and 3D layouts, so you can see how the furniture fits before you order. Planning often improves the result.

Example

A clean-tech manufacturer asked for 2 workbenches; our workspace analysis showed that 8 would spread its assembly stages better.

Do you install?

Yes. Our own team installs what we make, to the agreed scope. If you prefer to assemble Link Workstands yourself, we can supply them for self-assembly.

My floor is uneven. Will the bench rock?

No. Every bench has levelling glides. For rough or trimix floors, we fit threaded stud levellers (M12 x 25) with 10–15 mm of fine adjustment.

Do you ship outside India?

Yes. Benches ship knocked down, so they pack compactly into a container. For sea freight, each part is wrapped in corrugated board and shrink-wrapped, then palletised and crated.

Do you supply complete labs?

We supply the benches, storage, sink cabinets and work-area accessories in our range, and plan around the rest of your fit-out. Items such as safety showers and eye-wash stations come from specialist suppliers.

Example

We fitted out a 20 ft shipping container as a chemical lab, with stainless steel benches, power and lighting uprights, overhead cabinets, an HPL sink cabinet and a computer station.

Can I order the same setup I saw elsewhere?

Yes. Send us a photo of a Furnisys station you've seen, and we'll adapt it to your process.
