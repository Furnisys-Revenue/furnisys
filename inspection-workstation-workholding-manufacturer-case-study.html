A Clean, Organised Inspection Workstation for a Workholding Manufacturer

A Scitec Workstation with an SS-cladded worktop, levellers, footrest and a Link Uprighter with pegboard, tackboard, shelf, task light and power rail, replacing a cluttered inspection area.

Inspecting fixtures, clamps and lifting hardware

Situation

The client makes precision workholding and tooling systems: fixture components, clamping solutions and lifting hardware. Their inspection area uses arbor presses, laser marking machines, torque analysers, calipers, micrometers and leak test gauges.

Clutter, leaks and guidelines on the wall

The challenge

Work surfaces were cluttered. Fluid leaking from inspected components was caught on temporary rubber mats, which was neither sustainable nor hygienic. Inspection guidelines hung on the walls, away from the work. The client also needed better tool access, storage, lighting and power at the bench.

How do you make inspection clean and repeatable?

The question

How do you replace an improvised inspection area with a station that is easy to clean, keeps guidelines in front of the inspector, and puts every gauge within reach?

A Scitec Workstation built for inspection

The Furnisys approach

  • SS-cladded worktop that wipes clean of fluids, replacing the rubber mats
  • Rigid Scitec structure with levellers for alignment and a footrest for long sessions
  • Link Uprighter with a pegboard for calipers, micrometers and hand tools
  • Tackboard to mount inspection guidelines at the workstation
  • Adjustable shelf for gauges, fixtures and accessories, with an undershelf task light
  • Horizontal power rail for torque analysers and other inspection equipment

Guidelines, tools and power in one place

How the setup works together

The inspector reads the guideline on the tackboard, takes the gauge from the pegboard or shelf, measures under the task light, and wipes the SS top clean between parts.

What the client received

What the configuration enabled

  • A hygienic, easy-to-clean inspection surface
  • Guidelines at eye level, not on a distant wall
  • Tools, gauges, light and power at the point of inspection
  • A layout aligned with 5S

Inspection deserves a proper bench

Furnisys perspective

Inspection quality depends on consistency. Putting guidelines and gauges in fixed places at the bench is a simple way to get there.

Frequently asked questions

What worktop suits an inspection area with fluid leaks?

An SS-cladded worktop, which is easy to clean and hygienic compared with loose rubber mats.

Where should inspection guidelines go?

On a tackboard mounted at the workstation, so they are in front of the inspector during work.

What lighting suits precision inspection?

An undershelf task light mounted on the uprighter gives focused light on the measuring area.
