Compare the ways to buy workspace furniture.

There are four common ways to buy industrial and technical furniture: a fabricator, a product seller, an integrator or a configured system. Each has its place. Here is how they compare, so you can choose the right one for your project.

Discuss your projectSee how to buy

Compare the buying routes.

Furnisys system vs

Fabricator

Welded furniture made to a drawing for one site.

Where it works: Quick to start and built exactly to the drawing you hand over.

Where it falls short: Every change means cutting, welding or starting again, and quality depends on the workshop and the batch.

See the full comparison
Furnisys system vs

Product seller

Ready-made benches and cabinets from a catalogue.

Where it works: Stable quality and products you can see before you buy.

Where it falls short: Sizes and accessories are limited, and the products are not configured around your process.

See the full comparison
Furnisys system vs

Integrator

A project company that sources from several vendors.

Where it works: One contact for a whole project, often including layout and services.

Where it falls short: Build quality and future support depend on whichever vendors were used for that project.

Route by route, in detail.

Furnisys system vs fabricator

When a fabricator makes sense

  • You need a one-off item that no standard range covers
  • The layout will never change
  • The lowest day-one price matters more than anything after it

Where it falls short

  • Changing the layout means rework or new fabrication
  • Storage, accessories and benches rarely match
  • Quality and finish vary between batches and vendors
  • Little or no documentation, spares or after-sales

How a Furnisys system differs

  • Knock-down modules that are reconfigured, not rebuilt
  • Benches, storage and accessories designed as one system
  • Consistent build from our own 22,000 sq. ft. facility
  • Manuals, technical docs and standard spare parts

Furnisys system vs product seller

When a product seller makes sense

  • You need a few standard items quickly
  • The work is general and does not depend on the station
  • Nobody needs the pieces to work together as a line

Where it falls short

  • Limited sizes and accessories
  • Products are sold as items, not configured around the application
  • Pieces from different ranges do not connect
  • Expansion means buying again and hoping it matches

How a Furnisys system differs

  • A comprehensive range of sizes, worktops and accessories
  • Configured around the application and the process
  • Every module connects to the rest of the system
  • Add-on units extend what you already have

Furnisys system vs integrator

When a integrator makes sense

  • You need many trades managed under one contract
  • The project goes far beyond furniture
  • You have no plans to extend or change the setup

Where it falls short

  • Products come from several vendors, so consistency varies
  • Configurability depends on the sources used
  • Future changes depend on the same vendors being available
  • Support is project-based and ends with the project

How a Furnisys system differs

  • One manufacturer for frames, worktops, storage and accessories
  • Process-specific configuration from standard modules
  • The same range is available years later for additions
  • Design, manufacturing and installation from one team

Questions to ask before you buy.

Will it fit the work done here, not just the floor plan?

Ask whether the station is configured around the process, the tools and the parts handled, or simply sized to the room. Our configurations start from the application.

What happens when the process changes?

Ask how a layout is extended or rearranged, and what that costs. Furnisys systems add, remove and reposition modules instead of replacing benches.

Will every piece work together?

Ask whether benches, storage, trolleys and accessories come from one system. Ours share one frame, worktop and accessory standard.

Is the quality the same on every unit?

Ask where and how the furniture is made. Ours is manufactured in our own 22,000 sq. ft. facility in Navi Mumbai.

What support is there after installation?

Ask about manuals, spares and who to call. We provide user manuals, technical docs and standard spare parts.

Red flags to watch for.

Every change needs a new quote and a new build

If the supplier cannot reconfigure what they deliver, you will pay again each time the process changes.

Storage and benches from different sources

Pieces that were never designed together waste space and rarely line up.

Drawings, but no product range

Without standard modules, there are no spares, no add-ons and no consistency between batches.

A price with no lifecycle view

The cheapest day-one quote can be the most expensive layout after the first change.

No documentation

No manual, no part numbers and no technical data makes maintenance and audits harder.
