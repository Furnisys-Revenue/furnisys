Industrial Workstation RFQ Checklist: What to Send Suppliers and How to Compare Quotes

On this page
1. What to send every supplier2. What to ask every supplier for3. How to compare the quotes4. Before you sign the orderFrequently asked questions

Quotes for workstations are hard to compare when each supplier is answering a different question. Send every supplier the same brief, ask each one for the same documents, and compare what you will pay over the life of the line, not just the first quote.

1. What to send every supplier

  • Layout: a drawing, or photos with room dimensions, doors, columns and power points
  • Stations: how many, and what task each one is for
  • Specification per station: load in kg, size in mm, worktop, height, ESD yes or no, accessories, storage (the specification table has the full list)
  • Site: floor type, access for delivery, working hours for installation
  • Timeline: when you need it installed, and any shutdown window

2. What to ask every supplier for

Ask for the same items from everyone, so the quotes line up:

  • an itemised bill of quantities (BOQ), one line per module and accessory
  • 2D and 3D layout drawings of your space
  • load ratings in writing, per bench
  • materials and finish: frame, worktop, coating
  • delivery schedule and installation scope
  • warranty terms, written into the quotation
  • how spares and add-ons are ordered later
  • two or three reference installations similar to yours

3. How to compare the quotes

Put the quotes side by side, line by line, against your brief. Then check three things the total does not show:

  • What a change will cost. A one-off build may need cutting and welding every time the process changes. A modular system adds or moves modules.
  • Whether the parts were designed together. Benches, storage and accessories from different sources rarely line up.
  • What you get after delivery. Drawings, manuals, spares and support.

4. Before you sign the order

  • Approve the final drawings, and freeze changes after approval.
  • Confirm the delivery date, unloading, access and site readiness.
  • Agree who installs, and how the installation will be checked.
  • Keep the BOQ, drawings and manuals, so a later expansion can match the first order.

Frequently asked questions

What should an RFQ for industrial workstations include?

The layout, the number of stations and their tasks, the specification of each station (load, size, worktop, height, ESD, accessories, storage), site conditions and the installation timeline.

How do I compare workstation quotes fairly?

Ask every supplier for an itemised BOQ, layout drawings, load ratings in writing and warranty terms in the quote. Then compare line by line against the same brief, and include what later changes and spares will cost.

What is a BOQ for workstations?

A bill of quantities lists every bench, cabinet and accessory in the quote, with quantities and specifications. It is what makes two quotes comparable, and what a later expansion is matched against.
