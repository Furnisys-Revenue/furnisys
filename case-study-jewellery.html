Eleven process zones, one workstation system

A jewellery manufacturer setting up a new facility needed workstations for very different processes, from repetitive hammering to precise finishing. Furnisys configured two workstation families, base cabinets and mobile cabinets so every zone has the right setup.

Situation

The client manufactures gold, silver and designer ornaments such as bangles, chains and earrings, combining traditional craftsmanship with modern production. Their processes include melting, wire drawing, chain making, diamond cutting, plating and finishing.

With a new manufacturing facility under development, they wanted an organised production environment from day one.

The challenge

The new facility had eleven distinct zones: powder handling, wire drawing, tool room, diamond cutting, chain making, hammering, hallmarking, plating, tumbling, laser marking and R&D. The client wanted to avoid a facility where each zone ended up with improvised or mismatched furniture.

  • Hammering and mould work need high-impact, stable surfaces.
  • Some stations must carry mounted equipment such as surface grinders.
  • Frequently used tools need to be within reach and easy to remove.
  • Tools, consumables and components need safe storage in every department.
  • Maintenance teams need tools that move to the machine.

The question

How do you equip eleven different processes without designing eleven different benches?

Approach

  1. Pro Workstations for impact and heavy work

    Used in the tool room, diamond cutting, chain making and hammering sections.

    • Pro leg structure with base cabinets for strength and integrated storage
    • GI-cladded worktop to withstand hammering, die work and metal handling
    • Footrest for prolonged standing tasks
    • Link Uprighter starter and adder with a variable-angle shelf, pegboard, power supply modules, markerboard and a fixed tube light with reflector

  2. Scitec Workstations for lighter, process-oriented work

    Used in the powder area, wire drawing, tool room, chain making, hallmarking, plating, tumbling and laser marking sections.

    • Scitec leg structure with hanging drawers for light tools and measuring instruments
    • Postform laminated worktop for sorting, inspection, assembly and finishing
    • Instrument riser shelves with integrated power for small equipment
    • Rear pegboard and footrest

  3. Base Cabinets for structured storage

    Placed across wire drawing, tool room, diamond cutting, chain making, hammering, hallmarking, laser marking and R&D.

    • Heavy-duty telescopic slides rated up to 65 kg UDL
    • Forklift-compatible base so cabinets can be repositioned when the layout changes

  4. Mobile Cabinets for tools on the move

    Tool-on-the-go storage that brings tools to the point of use.

    • A compact 16" wide cabinet with a front handle for the space-constrained chain making area
    • 22" wide cabinets with more capacity for the other zones

How it works together

The two workstation families share the same logic: a leg structure, a worktop chosen for the process, storage below and an uprighter or riser above. Heavy zones get Pro stations and GI tops; precision zones get Scitec stations and laminate tops. Base cabinets hold each department's tools, and mobile cabinets connect the zones during maintenance.

What it enabled

  • Each process zone has a workstation matched to its tasks, inside one consistent system.
  • Frequently used tools are on pegboards and shelves at the station, not in shared stores.
  • Storage moves with the layout, through forklift-compatible base cabinets and mobile cabinets.
  • Maintenance teams can bring tools directly to equipment, which supports quicker interventions on the floor.

Furnisys perspective

A new facility is the right time to set a standard. Two workstation families covered eleven processes, and future zones can be added from the same components.

FAQ

Which workstation suits hammering and die work?

A Pro Workstation with a GI-cladded worktop. It is built for impact and load, and can carry a Link Uprighter for tools and power.

Can workstations carry mounted equipment?

Yes. Pro and Scitec configurations support mounted equipment such as surface grinders, and riser shelves hold small devices above the worktop.

What load can the base cabinet drawers take?

The base cabinets in this project used heavy-duty telescopic slides rated up to 65 kg UDL.
