A Heavy-Duty Maintenance Workbench, Shipped Overseas for a Soap Plant

A Pro Workbench with a GI-cladded top and rubber mat, configured for pump, valve and gearbox repair in a soap factory's maintenance zone and packed in knock-down form for sea freight.

A maintenance zone that keeps a soap line running

Situation

One of our aggregator partners brought us a requirement from an overseas soap manufacturer. The plant needed a heavy-duty worktable for its maintenance zone, the place where technicians repair, assemble and inspect the mechanical parts that keep mixers, moulding machines and conveyors running.

The work is unglamorous and constant: pump housings, gear units, motor brackets, valve assemblies, guiding rails, and the smaller parts that go with them, such as impellers, seals, bearings and couplings.

Heavy, oily parts on the bench, and an ocean between factory and site

The challenge

The bench had to carry substantial static loads and cope with parts that arrive oily or wet. Delicate components still needed a surface that would not scratch them or let them slide.

There was a second constraint that had nothing to do with the work itself. The bench would travel by sea, so it had to arrive complete and undamaged after weeks of handling, stacking and humidity.

How do you send a heavy-duty bench across the world without it arriving as a repair job?

The question

How do you configure a workbench strong enough for daily mechanical repair, then pack it so it survives international sea freight and goes together cleanly at the other end?

A Pro Workbench, configured for the work and packed for the journey

The Furnisys approach

1. Pro Workbench with GI-cladded worktop

We recommended the Pro Workbench, our heavy-duty bench for demanding industrial use. The worktop is GI (galvanized iron) cladded for strength and corrosion resistance, which matters when oily and wet parts land on it all day.

2. Rubber mat overlay

A rubber mat on the GI top gives a non-slip surface and protects machined faces on impellers, seals and bearings during detailed work.

3. Knock-down build for export

Because the Pro Workbench is built from standard modules (frame, worktop and structural supports), it can ship knocked down. Each part was wrapped individually in two-ply corrugated boxes and shrink-wrapped. The parts were pallet-nested for space and stability, then crated for the sea leg.

One bench for heavy repair and fine work

How the setup works together

The GI top takes the weight and the mess of pump and gearbox repair. The rubber mat turns the same surface into a safe place for small precision parts. The modular frame means the bench can be adjusted or extended later, and it is also what made compact export packing possible.

What the client received

What the configuration enabled

  • A maintenance bench rated for heavy static loads and frequent handling of oily parts
  • A worktop that resists corrosion and protects delicate components
  • A knock-down unit that shipped compactly and safely by sea
  • A modular base that can be extended as the maintenance zone grows

Logistics is part of the configuration

Furnisys perspective

For export projects, the way a system is packed is as important as the way it is built. Standard modules made it possible to deliver a heavy-duty bench to another continent without compromising on strength.

Frequently asked questions

What worktop suits a maintenance bench that handles oily parts?

A GI-cladded worktop resists corrosion and wipes clean easily. In this project a rubber mat was added on top to protect delicate components and stop parts from sliding.

Can a heavy-duty workbench be shipped overseas?

Yes. The Pro Workbench is built from standard modules, so it ships in knock-down form. Here each part was boxed, shrink-wrapped, pallet-nested and crated for sea freight.

Which parts was this bench used to repair?

Pump housings, gear units, motor brackets, valve assemblies and guiding rails, plus smaller parts such as impellers, seals, bearings and couplings used in soap mixers, presses and conveyors.
